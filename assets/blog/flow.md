I know this feeling from sport and coding. I have experienced it for years. What surprised me when reading Flow by Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi was that there is a word for it and that there are specific conditions that help us get into this state. Now AI has changed my work a lot and I don't have this feeling that often anymore. After reading the book, I want to reflect on where I find flow in my life. Here is my definition after reading:

**Flow:** The activity challenges us, but we have a good chance of completing it. We can concentrate, have a clear goal, and get immediate feedback on how we are doing. We feel in control of our actions. Even when the task takes effort, being involved in it can feel effortless. Everyday worries and frustrations temporarily disappear from our awareness. We also stop thinking so much about ourselves and how we appear to others. Afterwards, we can come away with a stronger sense of ourselves and our abilities (complexity of the self). The sense of time often changes too: hours can feel like minutes, or a brief moment can seem to stretch out.

That's it. So we need a real challenge. Not too difficult and not too easy for our capabilities, but with space for growth. We need a clear goal and something that gives immediate feedback in that specific activity.

Csikszentmihalyi describes this as 'optimal experience' and as one way to find more happiness in life. There is no unique recipe for happiness to copy. But his research suggests that flow can help. Relaxing on the couch gives us pleasure. With flow, we also develop our abilities. These activities often require some discomfort in the first place, although they can be truly enjoyable in the end.

## My flow experiences

The book explores a few different sources of flow: the body, thought, work, solitude, and other people. After reading it, I want to take the time to reflect on my personal flow activities and where I already experience this in my life.

I think it is important to not simply focus on one specific area and neglect the others instead. Having a good mix of flow activities across all areas looks like a good way to enjoy life in general.

<div class="section-break"></div>

### Body

This is probably the area where I have always naturally enjoyed flow, because I have been doing a lot of sports since early childhood.

No matter which sport, it is often something about *higher, faster, further*. Getting better gives us a goal and a challenge. In football, the goal is a bit different. Although it is with a team, you feel in control, and in training as well as matches you have immediate feedback on your performance. These are conditions for flow. The feeling itself happens while playing.

In the past I was more into playing football 3 times per week in a team competing against others throughout the year. In the meantime I have replaced playing football with running and strength training. In running, beating my best time in the next running event gives me a goal. In strength training, for me personally it is not about gaining muscle and looking in shape (this is just a nice byproduct), but about getting better in [my favorite exercises](/blog/my-favorite-full-body-workout-plan/): pull-ups, bench press and back squats.

Something that is not that developed for me yet but I want to focus more on is calisthenics. Not only being strong and having endurance, but also having control over my body. It is just impressive what our nervous system can do if we just train it. Consistently. I know that I find joy in practicing skills like pushing into a handstand and some other sophisticated calisthenics skills. It is just not yet a firm habit of mine.

Thinking about flow now, I would like to pick up my guitar again more often. Somehow this got lost during the last few years. I think priorities just pushed it down.

<div class="section-break"></div>

### Thought
Reading is probably the best-known one in this category. Also for me. We need to concentrate to follow an argument or understand a character. The book should challenge us, but we should still be able to understand it. I think understanding something that was not clear before is also a kind of feedback.

Writing in English is another one for me as a native German speaker. Using AI only to do some basic typo and grammar checks instead of heavy rephrasing. At least reducing it from time to time. Using my device in offline mode while writing the initial version.
Doing the often unpleasant task of thinking about some topic. Ordering thoughts while writing them down and making them concrete.

<div class="section-break"></div>

### Work

One finding from the book that I find interesting is that people in the studies often experienced more flow at work than in their free time. Nevertheless, they still wanted to work less and have more free time. I also wrote about this in [my thoughts on AI](/blog/my-thoughts-on-ai-09-2026/). Having free time does not automatically mean that we enjoy it more. I think about this when I spend an evening watching videos and am not really happy about it afterwards.

I am a software engineer and obviously coding has been my passion and a source of optimal experience. Headphones on, some slow techno music and the deep work can start. Breaking down bigger tasks into smaller problems, choosing the right data structure and implementing complex algorithms. Being happy after successful compilation, getting frustrated during debugging and finally having software running as intended. Coding fits what Mihaly describes as flow and optimal experience. At least it did for me.

Somehow this is gone. Currently it feels to me like coding is being solved by AI. At least my personal workflow has changed a lot in the agentic world. I am not doing much deep work anymore. Instead, I need to force myself not to grab my cellphone when agents are running.

For sure you can break it down to solving problems, and problem solving will also be there with AI. For sure it is also rewarding to launch a great product or just reach the next milestone, but it is on another abstraction level. Things now have broader, slower feedback like “the project moved forward.” For me, this does not give me the same enjoyment on a daily basis as coding did before.

Somehow this is really sad. Coding is fun, but in my free time I prefer other activities most of the time. At work, coding made the day really enjoyable for me. Coding at home would not change what I am missing during my working hours. I am not sure yet what can replace this.

I think understanding large code bases as well as complex functions and design patterns is still worth doing without AI support and could be as enjoyable as coding. Developers need to stay proficient in understanding code in the future (at least this is my personal opinion for now).

For now I don't really have an answer. Deep work with AI is different than before. When I am writing this post, I am not having this optimal experience that often at work anymore. The plan is to find flow in technical investigations, good meetings, design and review tasks, and unblocking colleagues. I want to come back to a state where I like my job in a similar way to before coding agents.

<div class="section-break"></div>

### Other people

Although I am an introvert and recharge my energy by being alone, I truly enjoy surrounding myself with people I like. Having good conversations or doing my favorite activities together. In a good conversation we need to listen and concentrate. The other person can challenge our thoughts and gives immediate feedback. I think this is why conversations can also be flow activities.

<div class="section-break"></div>

## Other examples from the book

My own activities only cover some of the examples in the book. He also writes about dancing, looking at art, listening to music, tasting food, memory, wordplay, poetry, history, science, and philosophy. Maybe some of these are more interesting to you. I think I am most interested in philosophy here. But I don't really have a flow activity there besides reading. I don't spend much time thinking about philosophical questions in depth.

He also writes about enjoying solitude. Without something to focus on, our thoughts can turn to worries. He connects this to 'psychic entropy', or disorder in consciousness. His point is that enjoying time alone can take practice in directing our attention.

<div class="section-break"></div>

## What I take from the book

Actually I have already incorporated lots of flow activities into my daily life without knowing the research behind them. I think in general I have a happy life and like most of the things I am doing. The book helped me understand more about why I enjoy these activities and what they have in common.

I still notice that I spend time on things I am not that happy about. Watching more YouTube videos when I am no longer getting useful information. Checking stock prices multiple times throughout the week although it is mostly noise. These are things I can pay attention to. Sometimes I also just want to rest. I don't want flow to become another thing I have to achieve every day.

At work I want to get back to a state where I enjoy daily work, not only the projects and products we are shipping. I don't really know yet how this will look with AI. But after reading the book, I understand a bit better what I am missing.

Maybe I already have plenty of flow in my life. Maybe there is no need to add more activities or spend more time on them. I can keep doing what I am doing, now with more awareness of when I experience flow and what helps me get there.
